demand-on-demand

Ask for a demand forecast in plain English. Get a tested forecast and dashboard in under a minute.

A business user types a question like "monthly forecast of Tito's minis in Des Moines for the next 5 months". One Claude agent turns the words into a precise request against a company-style Postgres warehouse of 26 million Iowa liquor orders (2016 to 2026). A fixed Python harness then builds the data, tunes and tests the models against the simplest honest benchmark, the same month last year, and publishes a dashboard that says how far to trust the answer.

How it works

  • The AI decides what to forecast; code decides how. The agent resolves products, places, measures and horizons with read-only tools, and hands over a spec. It knows the warehouse from a data dictionary of every table and column, read from the database. It never writes the SQL, the train/test split, the metrics or the charts. Those are fixed code, the same for every request.
  • Honest accuracy. Every forecast is backtested on the last 24 months, which no choice ever saw. Model settings are tuned on the two years before that, and "same month last year" is always a candidate: a model is used only as far as it beats it.
  • Clean data, fixed once. The public data has real problems: duplicated export rows, recoded categories, renumbered products, inconsistent spellings. They are documented on the data exploration page and fixed once, in the warehouse, as shown on the data fixes page. No forecast has to know they existed.
  • Least privilege. The agent's database login can only read the clean tables. Credentials stay in the Python process and never appear in a prompt.

Example forecasts

Five requests typed in plain English, each run once, shown as they came out, the misses included.

“Monthly forecast of Tito's minis in Des Moines for the next 5 months”

Des Moines Tito's minis are forecast at about 30,900 bottles over the next 5 months, up 7% from the same months last year. This forecast is 8% more accurate than simply repeating last year's outcome.

Backtest: 8% more accurate than repeating last year (0.92). 25 s, $0.02.
“What revenue should we expect from Fireball in Linn County next quarter?”

Fireball in Linn County is forecast to bring in about $553,000 next quarter, down 4% from the same months last year. This forecast is 9% more accurate than simply repeating last year's outcome.

Backtest: 9% more accurate than repeating last year (0.91). 39 s, $0.03.
“How many bottles of cream liqueur will Iowa stores order for the holidays?”

Iowa cream liqueurs are forecast at about 256,000 bottles through the holidays, down 3% from the same months last year. This forecast is 29% more accurate than simply repeating last year's outcome.

Backtest: 29% more accurate than repeating last year (0.71). 41 s, $0.02.

How good is it?

Forecast accuracy. On 30 forecasts sampled from the warehouse (products, categories, vendors; counties and statewide; bottles, dollars and liters; 3 to 12 months ahead), the chosen model beat "same month last year" on 23, with a median error 12% lower than that baseline (median monthly error 15.0% vs 16.0%). Retail demand here is very regular year to year, so last year is a hard baseline to beat, and on the rest it was not beaten. Full benchmark.

Agent accuracy. 42 of 42 runs across 21 test requests produced exactly the right product, place, measure and horizon, or correctly declined (a city outside Iowa, a 24-month horizon, a product too new to forecast). Every number in the dashboard summaries is checked against the harness's results. Eval report.

Slot filling vs AI-written SQL. The same requests were also given to an agent that writes the SQL itself (NL2SQL), and the two monthly histories compared month by month. Most matched exactly; the AI-written queries that did not ran fine and even matched the last 12 months, with the errors hidden in older history. The test and what it found.
