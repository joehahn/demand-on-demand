Every known problem in the published data: what it looks like, and how the loader fixes it once, in the warehouse. 4. Renumbered products

The state sometimes gives a product a new item number. Tito's 50 ml mini was item 38180 until July 2020 and 38194 after, so selecting today's number alone would lose four years of sales. The loader found 254 renumbered items (same description and size, the new number starting within weeks of the old one stopping) and links each old number to its product's current number, a product family, so the product's history is continuous. 5. A sleeve of minis counted as one bottle

The state records a sleeve of minis (e.g. Tito's 12 × 50 ml) as one "bottle". Its price per sleeve stayed about $13 (blue), but its recorded volume fell from the whole sleeve (about 700 ml) to one mini (about 50 to 70 ml) in mid-2019 (orange). So only the bookkeeping changed, not what stores bought. The dip near zero in late 2025 is a separate rounding error (section 6).

Where the pack size is known, the warehouse now counts each mini as a bottle and computes liters as bottles times one mini's volume, keeping the recorded values alongside. 2,109,660 records changed:

6. Liters rounded down to whole liters

From November 2025 to January 2026 the state rounded each record's liters sold down to a whole liter (bottle sizes were unaffected). A case of six 750 ml bottles (4.5 L) became 4 L, and a sleeve of 12 minis (0.6 L) became 0 L. Statewide that loses about 3.5% of liters in those months (405,713 records). Liters are now bottles times bottle volume on every record, which matches what was recorded in every other month.

7. Store details change, and cities are spelled several ways

462 of 3,199 stores changed name over the years, 272 address, 22 city and 5 county. A store keeps its number for life, so its sales stay together through these changes; the warehouse uses each store's most recent name, address and county. The 17 stores whose most recent records leave the county blank count in city and statewide requests but not county ones. Some cities are spelled more than one way, which would split one city into two; each now has one spelling:

9. Checked and left as is

Missing values. Blank values come and go by column and year. The state's bottle cost and retail price are blank before September 2025, which needs no fix because unit price is dollars divided by bottles. Blank counties affect a few percent of early records, and county comes from the store instead.

invoice_id changed meaning. Until August 2025 every order line had its own invoice_id; from September 1, 2025 one invoice_id covers a whole order, about 20 lines. Counting distinct invoice_ids would overstate orders about twentyfold before September 2025. Forecasts never use it: the warehouse gives every order line its own new ID, and the data dictionary warns that invoice_id identifies orders only from September 2025.

Large-volume records (6 liters or more) looked like errors but are genuine: pallet shippers and whole-cask purchases, kept as recorded.
